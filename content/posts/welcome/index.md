Hi! My name is Guilherme Rizzo, and in 2026 I decided to tackle some long-overdue tasks and finally act on plans I'd been putting off for a long time. Among them: organizing my digital files, updating and managing a financial spreadsheet, and learning a bit more about Linux, artificial intelligence, GitHub, and Python.

## Why a blog

This blog started as an extension of my [personal website](https://rizzoadm.github.io/), which I use as a companion to my professional resume. That's where my experience, skills, and projects live, in a more formal tone.

Here the idea is different: I want to share simpler content, with tests, failures, wins, and lessons learned, and talk about my experience without so much formality. Not everything will work on the first try, and that's exactly the point: to record the journey, not just the result.

## What I'll write about

The main topics will probably revolve around:

- homelab;
- artificial intelligence, both local and cloud;
- Linux, git, GitHub, and Python;

among other subjects I'm interested in.

## Posting schedule

There's no strict posting schedule. I don't want this blog to become an obligation. I want it to be a place where I write when I feel like it and have the time, focusing more on the joy of writing and sharing than on keeping up with a calendar.

## How to follow along

### Via RSS

RSS is a way to "subscribe" to a website without giving out your email or signing up here. You use an RSS reader app, which checks the sites you follow and shows new posts in one place.

1. Pick an RSS reader. Some free options: **Feedly** and **Inoreader** (browser and phone), **NetNewsWire** (Mac and iPhone), and **Thunderbird** (desktop).
2. Copy the blog's address:
   - in English: `https://rizzoadm.github.io/blog/index.xml`
   - in Portuguese: `https://rizzoadm.github.io/blog/pt-br/index.xml`
3. In the reader, look for the option to add a site or feed (usually "Add feed", "Follow", or a "+" button) and paste the address.

That's it: whenever a new post goes up, it shows up in your reader.

### Via comments

At the end of each post there's a comments section. To comment, you need a GitHub account (it's free). Comments are shared between the English and Portuguese versions, so the whole conversation stays in one place.
